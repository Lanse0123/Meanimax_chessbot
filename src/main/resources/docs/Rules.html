World War Chess Rules

Note: this is not meant to be a comprehensive set of rules, it should be just enough to get you playing a few games, at which point you can explore on your own.

Summary

Most of the rules of chess are the same, except where they needed to be changed to make multiple boards work. In general, each board is controlled by 1 player, who gets to move 1 of their pieces each turn. When their king dies, the game isn't over, but all their pieces die - so they can only watch and hope that their teammates can still win.

Turns

There are several modes: humans vs. humans, humans vs. NPC and NPC vs. NPC. In all of these modes, everyone on one team has a fixed amount of time that they can make one move in. When everyone on the current team has moved (or the timer has run out), the other team starts taking their moves.

On their turn, each player can move one of the pieces that started on their board (although NPCs by default can move two of their pieces). Legal moves now include moving onto other boards (even non-adjacent ones), although for pieces moving in a line (bishops, rooks and queens) every intermediate square must be on some board.

Commander Pieces

Every team has 1 commander board: a board with boss pieces 4 times as large as normal ones. Killing your opponents commander king is how you win the entire game.

Commander pieces can capture other commander pieces like normal, but it's harder for normal pieces to capture them. Commanders and boss pieces have health bars that get decreased by a set amount when they are attacked by a normal piece. If multiple pieces attack a commander at the same time, their combined damage gets a boost for each attacker piece.

Additionally, commander pieces can capture normal pieces quickly: they capture all the pieces in any of the squares they are moving into (however, this does mean that one of your regular pieces can block your commander pieces, which can make it hard for them to get past your own team, though.)

Miscellaneous Differences and Clarifications

  • Pawns promote when reaching the end of any board.
  • The game isn't over when a king dies, only when a Commander King dies.
  • You are allowed to make a move leaving or putting your king in check (even for commander kings).
  • Commander kings cannot castle.
  • En-passant does not work.
